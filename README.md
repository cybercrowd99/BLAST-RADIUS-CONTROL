Blast-Radius Control (BRC)

Overview

Blast-Radius Control (BRC) is a structural containment architecture designed to keep distributed systems stable by enforcing boundaries around movement, mutation, authority, and failure propagation.

BRC defines the structural rules that allow systems to evolve without uncontrolled expansion.

BRC is not a feature, service, application, or product layer.

BRC is a governing structural model for maintaining system sovereignty.

---

Problem Definition

Distributed systems fail when change travels farther than intended.

Every system grows.

Every system changes.

Every system can leak unless boundaries exist.

Without containment:

- Functions become uncontrolled mutation paths.
- Modules become dependency expansion points.
- Layers become uncontrolled blast radiuses.
- Authority can transfer without permission.
- Evidence chains can become corrupted.

BRC exists to prevent uncontrolled movement.

---

Structural Truth

A system's integrity is not preserved by complexity.

It is preserved by containment.

A system without boundaries becomes unstable.

Containment is the foundation that allows controlled evolution.

---

BRC Identity

Blast-Radius Control (BRC) is a structural containment architecture.

BRC defines the boundaries that allow distributed systems to evolve without uncontrolled mutation, authority transfer, dependency expansion, or failure propagation.

BRC is not a feature, service, application, or product layer.

BRC is a governing structural model for maintaining system sovereignty.

BRC identifies every system component by:

- Responsibility
- Authority
- Boundary
- Movement
- Containment

A component exists within a defined radius.

Within that radius, change is permitted.

Beyond that radius, change requires an explicit boundary crossing.

BRC preserves separation between:

- Presentation
- Execution
- Authority
- Continuity
- Communication

BRC establishes that stability does not come from preventing change.

Stability comes from ensuring that change remains controlled.

The identity of BRC is:

A system remains survivable when every movement has a boundary, every authority has an owner, and every change has a contained radius.

---

BRC Scope

BRC governs structural containment across:

- Repositories
- Services
- Layers
- Interfaces
- System boundaries

BRC does not govern:

- Business logic
- Product direction
- Domain behavior

BRC defines how systems remain separated while allowing controlled movement.

Core Principle

Movement is allowed.

Uncontrolled movement is not.

---

Containment Model

Surface Boundary

Protects public interfaces and presentation layers.

Controls:

- Public access points
- User-facing surfaces
- External presentation

Surface cannot mutate deeper execution systems.

---

Engine Boundary

Protects internal processing and execution systems.

Controls:

- Logic execution
- Operations
- Internal mutation

Engine cannot mutate authority structures.

---

Authority Boundary

Protects ownership, trusted decisions, and verified operations.

Controls:

- Ownership
- Permissions
- Contracts
- Authority records

Authority cannot transfer itself across boundaries.

---

Vault Boundary

Protects deep continuity structures.

Controls:

- Origin references
- Historical records
- Continuity chains
- Protected state

Vault cannot mutate external behavior.

---

Adapter Boundary

Controls communication between systems.

Adapters:

- Translate
- Present declared outputs
- Connect controlled interfaces

Adapters do not:

- Create authority
- Rewrite protected records
- Bypass containment

---

Boundary Rules

Each layer begins where its responsibility begins.

Each layer ends where its authority ends.

A component may operate inside its declared boundary.

A component may not silently expand beyond its authority.

---

Mutation Control

Mutation must remain:

- Local
- Predictable
- Declared
- Contained

A mutation outside its boundary becomes structural drift.

---

Dependency Leakage Prevention

Dependencies must remain within their declared radius.

A dependency cannot silently expand authority.

A connection does not create ownership.

Access does not create control.

---

Authority Preservation

- Drift
- Transfer accidentally
- Be inherited without declaration
- Be created by external communication

Every authority must have an owner.

---

Evidence Chain Protection

- Sealed
- Traceable
- Protected
- Uncorrupted

Continuity depends on preserving origin and record integrity.

---

Sovereignty Definition

- Responsibility
- Authority
- Continuity
